Interviewer presses further — say these out loud

Cover before you open. This lab is short on clicks and long on judgment — that’s intentional.

Q1. Why not put every dbt model behind a Snowflake Task?

Say: Tasks run SQL in Snowflake; dbt is a client that compiles a graph, runs tests, and needs git/CI context. Tasks are perfect for stream consume. For medallion dbt I’d trigger dbt from Airflow or dbt Cloud, not rewrite the project as one giant task body.

Q2. Airflow vs dbt Cloud schedule — when do you pick each?

Say: dbt Cloud when the company standard is dbt-native jobs and the DAG is mostly dbt. Airflow when files, APIs, tickets, and dbt must share one retry/SLA story. Meridian overnight file → bronze → dbt gold is Airflow-shaped.

Q3. Where do retries and backfills live?

Say: Orchestrator retries the operator; dbt incremental/backfill semantics live in the model (watermark, --full-refresh). Don’t confuse Airflow retry of a failed dbt run with fixing a bad unique_key.

Q4. How do you avoid Airflow and a Task both writing SILVER_TXNS?

Say: One writer per table. CDC task owns TXN_SILVER_CDC; dbt owns SILVER_TXNS / GOLD_CLIENT_SUMMARY. Document the contract on the whiteboard — two writers is how Marcus gets two truths.

Q5. You don’t have Airflow in the trial account — are you lying in the interview?

Say: No — I prove dbt and Snowflake hands-on; I design the Airflow DAG on the whiteboard with sensors, order, retries, and alerts. Trial limits don’t excuse a missing orchestration story.

Elena’s wrap-up (say this out loud)
“Transforms live in dbt; Snowflake holds the floors; something must wake the graph. CDC stays on Tasks; overnight file → bronze → dbt silver/gold → notify is an Airflow DAG with retries and an owner. I don’t run production dbt from a laptop cron, and I don’t wrap every Task in Airflow. Outcome: Marcus knows who pages when gold is late.”
