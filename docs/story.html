SnowShop

A small online store. Real customer secrets. A boss who wants answers yesterday. You are not memorizing menus — you are living one week inside this company. Each lab is the next day of the same story.

How to use this page Read a chapter here → then open that lab and run the SQL. When something feels abstract, come back and re-read the chapter. The people stay the same the whole way.

The cast (memorize these five)

Who’s who
  • Maya — Snowflake Architect (that’s you). Builds the workshop. Designs the locks. Explains the “why”.
  • Priya — CEO of SnowShop. Wants speed, safety, and a bill that doesn’t explode.
  • Alex — Business Analyst. Needs charts. Must not see SSN or full emails.
  • Jordan — Data Engineer. Lives in messy dock data. Builds pipes and dbt models.
  • Sam — Compliance Officer. Allowed to see sensitive fields for audits — and only for that.

Later you’ll meet Nora (partner store that needs a data share) and Dev (QA twin of production).

Why badges are named FR_ and AR_ (not random)

Naming rule Maya uses forever

Snowflake lets you name roles anything. Maya refuses random names. She uses prefixes so a tired human can read a list at 2 a.m. and still know what a badge is for.

PrefixFull wordsMeansExample
FR_ Functional Role A job people wear — who you are at work FR_ANALYST = Alex’s job badge
AR_ Access Role A key-ring — what doors that job may open (no humans wear this alone) AR_CURATED_READ = “read the clean shelves”

Rule of the story: clip key-rings (AR_) onto job badges (FR_). Never hand every table key to every person by name. Hire fifty Alexes tomorrow — same job badge, same rings.

The week at SnowShop

00

Monday morning — Maya gets a workshop

Priya walks into Maya’s desk with coffee and panic. “We’re drowning in spreadsheets. Build us a real data home. Small bill. No drama.”

Maya creates a private workshop building named ARCH_INTERVIEW, rents a tiny generator WH_ARCH_XS that sleeps when idle, and pins a manager badge on herself: ROLE_ARCH_LAB_ADMIN. She labels rooms: RAW (loading dock), CURATED (clean shelves), SECURE (policy office), MART (report wall), STAGE_DATA (truck bay), UTILS (toolbox).

Before lunch she drops five fake customers into the dock vault — names, emails, SSN last-4. The vault exists. The story can begin.

Do Lab 00

01

Monday afternoon — three jobs, one vault

Alex wants customer names for a board deck. Jordan needs the messy vault to build pipelines. Sam needs SSN for an audit. Priya says: “If Alex ever sees SSN, we all get fired.”

Maya doesn’t invent fifty personal keys. She prints:

  • FR_ANALYST, FR_DATA_ENGINEER, FR_COMPLIANCE — job badges
  • AR_CURATED_READ, AR_RAW_READ, AR_PII_READ — key-rings

She installs a glass reception window V_CUSTOMERS_PUBLIC (no email, no SSN). Alex’s job gets only the curated key-ring. Jordan gets vault + curated. Sam gets PII + curated.

Maya puts on Alex’s badge and tries the vault door. It refuses. She smiles. That refusal is the product.

Do Lab 01

02

Tuesday — Jordan’s night clerk (dbt)

Orders keep arriving. Priya asks Jordan: “Do you rewrite the entire order book every night?” Jordan looks offended. “Only new pages and corrections.”

Maya and Jordan wire dbt: Day 1 five orders land in RAW. The clerk builds a clean fact table. Day 2 two new orders appear; order 1001 ships. The clerk merges — no duplicate junk, no full rewrite. Alex will eventually chart from the clean book, not the dock clipboard.

Do Lab 02

03

Wednesday morning — magic ink and velvet ropes

Sam is still nervous. “Even on the clean table, Alex might see full emails.” Maya paints emails with magic ink: Alex sees ***@…; Sam sees the real address. She adds velvet ropes: Alex only walks US aisles; Sam can walk every country.

Same SELECT. Different costume. Different reality. Priya finally exhales.

Do Lab 03

04

Wednesday afternoon — dock, kitchen, dining room

Priya tours the building and gets lost. Maya draws three zones on a napkin: Bronze dock (RAW), Silver kitchen (cleaned CURATED), Gold dining room (MART summaries for Alex).

A visiting consultant whispers “Data Vault.” Maya nods: “That’s another kitchen layout — great for huge history and audit trails. SnowShop doesn’t need a cathedral yet.” Priya cares about the napkin, not the buzzword.

Do Lab 04

05

Thursday — conveyor belt, sous-chef, recipe book

Jordan is tired of hand-carrying boxes from dock to kitchen. Maya shows three helpers:

  • Stream + task — a conveyor belt that moves new boxes on a schedule
  • Dynamic table — a sous-chef who keeps one dish fresh with a promised lag
  • dbt — the recipe book the team versions in Git (Tuesday’s clerk, grown up)

They start the belt, prove one box moves, then switch the belt off so the generator doesn’t hum all night.

Do Lab 05

06

Thursday evening — trucks at the bay

A truck backs into STAGE_DATA. Files spill onto the bay. COPY INTO pours them into a landing table. Priya asks, “Can this happen while we sleep?” Maya: “That’s Snowpipe — same pour, automatic when the cloud door beeps. Tonight we practice the pour by hand so you understand the machine.”

Do Lab 06

07

Friday morning — X-ray for a slow order

Alex’s dashboard crawls. Priya is already drafting an angry Slack. Maya opens Query Profile — the X-ray. Where did time go? Spilling? Waiting? She teaches the difference: bigger generator (scale up) vs more generators for a crowd (scale out). Alex’s query breathes again. Priya deletes the draft.

Do Lab 07

08

Friday noon — label the aisles

The event shelf has grown. Jordan walks every aisle for “last seven days.” Maya clusters by date — labels the aisles so the walk is short. She warns Sam about a fancy “search optimization” index: brilliant for needle-in-haystack lookups, not free candy.

Do Lab 08

09

Friday afternoon — Dev the twin and the undo button

Jordan deletes a table by accident. The room goes quiet. Maya presses UNDROP. Life returns. Then she introduces Dev — a zero-copy twin of a table for QA experiments. “Practice on Dev,” she says. “Don’t juggle production china.”

Do Lab 09

10

Saturday — Nora wants a window, not a USB stick

Nora runs a partner store. She asks for a data dump. Maya refuses the USB stick era. She shares a secure view — Nora sees only what SnowShop allows, live. Priya eyes the Marketplace like a mall of such windows for the future.

Do Lab 10

11

Saturday evening — what if the city floods?

Priya asks the hard question: “If our region dies, do we die?” Maya draws three answers on the whiteboard: clone for practice, share for partners, replicate/failover for a second city that keeps selling. SnowShop chooses on purpose — not after the flood.

Do Lab 11

12

Sunday — write the kitchen rules

Jordan’s dbt recipes are spreading. Maya adds contracts: “This dish must keep these columns.” Slim CI: only cook what changed in the pull request. Alex stops fearing silent breakages. The recipe book is finally a team sport.

Do Lab 12

13

Sunday night — the electric meter

Finance forwards a credit bill. Priya’s eye twitches. Maya opens the meter (ACCOUNT_USAGE), finds which generator hummed, and bolts on a resource monitor — a breaker that trips before the house burns. “Architects own cost,” she says, “not just diagrams.”

Do Lab 13

14

Interview Monday — tell SnowShop out loud

No Snowsight in the room. Just Maya (you) across from someone who asks: How do you stop Alex from seeing SSN? Why merge not rebuild? Spill on Query Profile? Share or replicate?

You don’t recite syntax. You tell this week. You are the architect who lived it.
